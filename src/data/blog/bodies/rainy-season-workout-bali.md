## Why the rainy season quietly breaks good routines

Bali's wet season usually arrives around November and stays for several months. Mornings are often bright, but heavy showers can come in fast, roads get slower, and some streets hold water after a downpour. None of that is a crisis. It just adds friction.

And friction is what stops training. A sunrise run along the beach gets rained out. The ride to the gym takes twice as long, so you skip it "just this once". An outdoor class is cancelled, and by the end of the week you have done nothing at all. For people living in Bali, the wet months can turn into a long pause. For visitors, a two-week trip can pass without a single proper session.

The fix is not more willpower. It is removing the trip. When the coach comes to you and the session happens under a roof, the weather no longer gets a vote.

## How in-house training changes the wet months

In-house training simply means the professional comes to where you already are: a villa, a family home, a long-stay rental or a hotel with permission. You do not travel, you do not get soaked on a scooter, and you do not wait for a gap in the clouds.

What that changes in practice:

- **The session stays on.** A covered terrace, an open-air pavilion (bale), a living room or a villa gym is enough. Rain on the pool becomes background noise, not a reason to cancel.
- **The time works for you.** Book the early window before the afternoon storms, or train while the rain falls. Either way, the hour is fixed and the coach is on the way.
- **You keep momentum.** Two or three fixed sessions a week give the whole week a shape, which is exactly what tends to disappear when it rains every afternoon.
- **You learn a home routine.** Each session can end with a few movements you repeat on your own between visits, so you are never starting from zero.

Many people find the wet months feel better when they move regularly. You may sleep more easily and feel less stiff from days spent indoors, and the grey afternoons feel shorter when you have already trained.

## Where a rainy-day session can happen

You do not need a home gym. You need a dry, level space with enough room for a mat and a couple of steps in each direction. Good options include:

- **A covered terrace or veranda** with a roof that keeps the floor dry when the rain blows sideways.
- **An open-air bale or pavilion**, the classic Bali villa space, ideal for strength work, yoga or Pilates.
- **A living room or bedroom** with the furniture moved back for an hour.
- **A villa or hotel gym**, if one is on site and you are allowed to use it.

Tiles and timber can get slippery when wet, so the coach checks the floor first and keeps the session to the dry part of the space. If you are not sure your place is suitable, our guide on [how to set up a villa for a workout](/resources/train-in-a-bali-villa) walks through simple layouts.

## Build a simple home routine for the wet months

The sessions with a coach are the anchor. What you do between them keeps the habit alive. Ask your coach to leave you a short plan you can do in 20 to 30 minutes in the same space, with no equipment or just a band. A simple week might look like this:

- **Two or three coached sessions** at fixed times, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday mornings.
- **One or two short solo sessions** from the plan your coach gave you, on the days in between.
- **A daily ten-minute stretch** in the evening, when the rain keeps you indoors anyway.

Keep it realistic. A routine you can repeat in a small, dry space is worth far more than an ambitious plan that depends on sunshine. Write your session times down like meetings, and protect them.
