## Why book a yoga teacher at your villa instead of a studio class

Bali is full of beautiful yoga studios, and many are worth a visit. But a holiday has a rhythm of its own. A studio class means checking a timetable, booking a scooter or a car, sitting in traffic, finding parking and sharing the room with twenty strangers. By the time you are on the mat, the calm sunrise you pictured has turned into a busy morning.

When the teacher comes to you, the whole hour belongs to you. You can practise in loose clothes you slept in, step straight from the mat into the pool and have breakfast ready when you finish. Nobody is watching, nobody is rushing, and the teacher can slow down, repeat a pose or skip one entirely because the class is built for one or two people, not a full room.

In-villa yoga in Bali also works well for people who feel shy in group classes. Complete beginners, people coming back to yoga after a long break, and travellers who just want to stretch out a stiff back after a long flight often find a private hour far more comfortable than a studio class.

## How to prepare your villa for a yoga teacher

You do not need a dedicated yoga deck. A few simple things make the hour smoother:

- **Choose a dry, flat, shaded spot.** A covered terrace, a bale or a living room with tile or wood floors works well. A wet deck in the midday sun does not.
- **Plan an indoor back-up.** Bali rain can arrive quickly, so pick a room where two mats fit if the weather turns.
- **Let your host know.** Many villas ask guests to tell staff or security when an outside professional is visiting. A quick message to your villa manager avoids any delay at the gate.
- **Share arrival details.** A map pin, gate code or parking note in your WhatsApp message helps your teacher arrive on time.
- **Ask about mats and props.** Mats and agreed props are confirmed on your quote, so you know in advance what your teacher brings.
